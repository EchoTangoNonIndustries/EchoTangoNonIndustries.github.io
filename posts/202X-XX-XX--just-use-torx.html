Please Stop Making Easily-Strippable Screw-Heads

Here's my latest idea to improve the world: use Torx screw-heads for all screws in consumer goods. They've been around since 1967, and it would make our lives all slightly less annoying if they replaced all the other bad ones.

Flat-head screws are a complete joke in a world with inexpensive battery-powered mini-screwdrivers, ubiquitous smart-watches and smart-phones, and self-driving cars. In addition to cam-out, they don't keep your driver centered in the screw. Luckily, the only recent sightings of them in new products, has been the wall-plates I was replacing for light-switches and electrical outlets in my house. Although those plates are only about $7 for a ten-pack at the hardware store, I'm pretty sure bumping the price to even $10, for a massive mark-up on the increased cost of the replaced screws, would be totally fine. Because nobody's replacing their wall-plates frequently enough to care about the cost. (And contractors' time fucking around with slipping drivers is worth more when building new.)

Phillips screws are better, but still unjustifiable in the modern world. Although Phillips didn't originally design them to cam out, after he fucked up the initial design he kept the cam-out because factories didn't have another way to easily stop after the screws had been tightened fully. That's a nice bit of history, but torque-limiting drivers are cheap enough for me to have one on my personal electric drill / driver. All factories have them too, because I don't remember a single time that I've seen cam-out marks on a screw in a newly purchased thing.

As much as I like waving my nice, patriotic Canadian flag around, Robertson screws are not enough of an improvement for me to actually enjoy. They don't self-center as nicely as Phillips, and the screw-heads are always shallow enough that I need to keep the driver perfectly aligned to avoid stripping the head. (Shallow screw heads hurt everything, but in my experience with consumer goods, Robertson screw-heads are slightly worse here.) Into the metal-recycling bin, please!

Hexagon-hole screw-heads are sort of the worst shape you could possibly have. They strip more easily than Robertson because there's less rounding-out needed to turn them into useless circles. Having six angles instead of four helps when tightening screws in odd places, such as cheap, assemble-it-yourself furniture, but Torx also has that! Please move this into the museum!

The vast majority of consumer goods could use Torx. My laptop uses Torx screws, and I needed my glasses to check that! Those screw-heads are about the same size as the Phillips one on my mini soldering iron, and the screws on the last wrist-watch I bought. (I was actually buying it just for the strap, since I couldn't find official straps for my Bangle JS 2 online at the time.) Now, at that small size they'd have to pay the licensing fees and follow the actual, stringent specs, but...that just means we'd have watches that are more easily repaired. Larger items are even easier - even with the knock-offs, your screws probably won't have stripped heads.

In addition to having less stripped screws, we'd all have another benefit - we could all have smaller or lighter screw drivers. In an imagined future with only Torx, your multi-bit driver can have more sizes in that handle, rather than the two or maybe three most common sizes of flat, Robertson and Phillips. For stronger or larger non-multi drivers, our tool-boxes could be smaller too. I'm certain the slight increased cost of screws would still be a net gain, since we'd all just spend slightly more on consumer goods but save money on drivers and tool-boxes.

In summary, I'm sick of non-Torx screws. The world's companies, governments and citizens should unite, buy out the trademark for Torx, and throw everything else into the fires of hell metal-recycle bins.
